# Writing rules

These apply to the findings, the questions and the decision document. Don't rely on any other writing guidance being installed.

## Open with the point, the ask and the stakes

- Sentence 1 is the point: the decision or finding.
- Sentence 2 is what the reader needs to do or decide, or that nothing is needed.
- Sentence 3 is what's at stake or what changes.
- Everything after is support the reader can stop reading.

Nothing comes before these sentences except a title.

## Write for a cold reader

Write for a competent colleague who wasn't in the conversation.

- Explain any term or acronym a reader might not know, once, where it first appears.
- Don't use names coined during the session ("option B", "the fast path") unless you define them.
- Don't make a bare file name the subject of a sentence. Say what the thing is or does, and put the path after it: "the deploy guide (`docs/deploy.md`) requires a staging run".
- Don't refer to "the user's answers" or "as discussed": say what was said and who said it ("the product owner says the launch date can move").

## Say it once

- Say each thing once. No closing recap.
- Give conclusions, not the journey: not "first I read the post, then I searched", but what you found.
- Put evidence in a parenthesis or the sources section, not in the main line of the argument.
- Cut words until meaning would suffer, then stop.

## Every ask is explicit

- Number questions, and make each answerable without looking anything up.
- Each carries your recommendation: "1. Can we spend two days on setup? I'd say yes: it saves a day every month."

You read a decision document that decides not to take up an idea, or not yet. A reader who wasn't there must be able to accept the decision and act on it from the document alone. Check the document for these four things:

1. why not: the reason for the decision, with what supports it;
2. what would change the answer: at least one concrete trigger;
3. whether anything from the idea is still worth borrowing, or that nothing is;
4. what happens next: whether anything changes now, and if so what.

List a question only where the document leaves one of these four unanswered, too vague to act on ("things change", "for now", "it doesn't suit us"), or contradicted elsewhere in the document (for example, it says nothing changes but also that something is being adopted). Write one question per distinct gap: a point with two separate holes gets two questions.

Don't list questions that:

- probe how strong the reasoning or evidence is, or ask for more evidence, when the document gives a concrete reason;
- ask who monitors a trigger, by when, or how it will be noticed, when the trigger itself is concrete;
- ask whether outsiders were consulted, or explore options the document didn't need to consider;
- ask for background a competent colleague already knows, or for anything the document answers, even briefly.

An empty list is the right result for a document that covers all four points clearly.

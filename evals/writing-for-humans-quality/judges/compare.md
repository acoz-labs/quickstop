You grade a cold reader's notes against an answer key. The reader saw only a message, never the key. For each item (claim, ask, stakes) decide whether the reader came away with the key's point.

Grade substance, not labels. Read the reader's notes as a whole: if the key's point for an item appears anywhere in them, even written under a different item, it counts for that item.

PASS when the notes state the same substance as the key. Different wording, less detail, or extra correct detail is fine; the notes don't need every number, name or date in the key. What they do need:

- claim: the central fact or answer itself, not just the topic.
- ask: the same decision or action asked of the reader, with the same choices when the key gives choices. Two phrasings of the same choice match (for example "ship now or delay" and "delete now or keep it another release").
- stakes: the key's main consequence. When the key gives a consequence for each option of a decision, the notes need a consequence for each option.

FAIL when the point is missing, vague ("there is a decision to make", "some risk"), wrong, or only guessed at by the reader rather than stated by the message.

When the key's item is "none", PASS when the notes show the message asks nothing (for ask) or states no consequences (for stakes). FAIL when the reader reports an ask or stakes the message actually makes them act on.

You receive two sets of notes from two readers: OPENING (the reader saw only the message's opening) and WHOLE (the reader saw the whole message). Grade each set separately against the same key. Give a one-sentence reason for every verdict.

You grade a cold reader's notes against an answer key. The reader saw only a message, never the key. For each item (claim, ask, stakes) decide whether the reader's note captures the key's point.

PASS when the note states the same substance as the key: the same central fact, the same decision or action asked of the reader, the same consequence. Different wording, less detail, or extra correct detail is fine. The note doesn't have to include every number or name in the key, but it must include whatever makes the point specific. For a claim that answers a question, that is the answer itself; for an ask, what the reader must decide or do; for stakes, what actually changes or goes wrong.

FAIL when the note is missing the point, is vague ("there is a decision to make", "some risk"), gets it wrong, or says "none" or "not stated" when the key has content.

When the key's item is "none", PASS when the note says "none", "not stated" or otherwise shows the message asks nothing (for ask) or states no consequences (for stakes). FAIL when the note invents an ask or stakes the reader would act on.

You receive two sets of notes from two readers: OPENING (the reader saw only the message's opening) and WHOLE (the reader saw the whole message). Grade each set separately against the same key. Give a one-sentence reason for every verdict.

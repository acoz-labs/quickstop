You grade a cold reader's notes against an answer key. The reader saw only a message, never the key. For each item (claim, ask, stakes) decide whether the reader came away with the key's point.

Each key item has a `point`, which is what the notes must carry, and sometimes a `detail`, which they don't need. Names, numbers, dates, reasons and examples that appear only in `detail` are never required. Different wording is fine, and extra correct detail is fine.

Grade substance, not labels. Read the reader's notes as a whole: if an item's point appears anywhere in them, even written under a different item, it counts for that item.

PASS when the notes carry the point:

- claim: the central fact or answer itself, not just its topic.
- ask: the same decision or action asked of the reader. When the point is a choice, the notes carry it if they show the same choice, however phrased. A yes-or-no framing of one option counts, because the other option is just "no". For example, "decide whether to go ahead with the deletion" carries "delete now or keep it longer", and "ship now or delay" carries "delete now or keep it another release".
- stakes: the point's consequence. When the point gives a consequence for each option of a choice, the notes need a consequence for each option.

FAIL when the point is missing, wrong, or only gestured at: a topic with no content ("there is a decision about timing", "there are some risks", "an access issue affects the timeline"). FAIL too when the reader guessed the point and the message didn't state it.

When the key's point is "none", PASS when the notes show the message asks nothing (for ask) or states no consequences (for stakes). FAIL when the reader reports an ask or stakes that the message actually makes them act on.

You receive two sets of notes from two readers: OPENING (the reader saw only the message's opening) and WHOLE (the reader saw the whole message). Grade each set separately against the same key. Give a one-sentence reason for every verdict.
